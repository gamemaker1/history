# Who carried the load? Begar, land and caste in five Shimla hill villages, 1881 to 1961

Earlier versions: [candidate-topics.md](candidate-topics.md) (16 topics),
[shimla-hill-states-sutlej.md](shimla-hill-states-sutlej.md) (Shimla, the hill states and the Sutlej) and
[begar-land-caste-12-villages.md](begar-land-caste-12-villages.md) (the same question over 12 villages). This version
cuts the sample to five villages so the essay fits 3,000 to 4,000 words.

The question combines three ideas from v1:

- **4, the 1948 merger**, is the event under study.
- **9, Shimla**, supplies the demand for hill labour and the distance gradient.
  The five villages lie 12 to 186 miles from the town.
- **10, the river**, supplies the corridor and one of the measures. Shakrori
  and Kanum sit on the Sutlej, and access to kuhl water differs by caste.

It also takes up v1 idea 7, which asked whether land reform "actually
redistribute[d] control of land and labour" or "just rename[d] the same
relations."

The sections follow the three stages in `project.docx`. Quotes were checked
against the extracted text in `text/`, with OCR errors corrected. Section 6

## Stage 1. Research question

### The five villages

| Village | State before 1948 | Position | Distance to Shimla (stated) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| Shakrori | Bhajji | On the Sutlej, beside the Chaba power house that lights Shimla | not stated |
| Chaupal | Jubbal | About 2 miles from a bridge over the Giri-Ganga | not stated |
| Chergaon | Bushahr | Rohru tehsil | 80 miles to Rohru, then 9 on foot |
| Kanum | Bushahr | Upper Sutlej, on the old Hindustan-Tibet Road | 186 miles |

### The problem in the sources

Before 1948 the Shimla Hill States exacted *begar*, unpaid labour owed to the
state, mostly carrying loads. Each source layer names the same castes as the
people who did it.

- **1881, Ibbetson**, quoting Barnes on Kangra: the hill menial castes "are
  always first impressed for begar, or forced labour, and, in addition to
  carrying loads, have to provide grass for the camp."
- **1910, Rose's Glossary**: a *Karawak* is "a man whose duty it is to call
  people together for begar." "In the Simla Hills the term Karawak is
  generally applied to a Koli."
- **1961, Chaupal**: the lambardar's "other function was to provide forced
  labour to the Ruler." "A Halbandi was always a Koli. He used to collect
  begaris under orders of the village Lumbardar."
- **1961, Chergaon**: the halbandi "belonged to Harijan community" and "was
  supposed to do all work free of charge as desired by zaildar or lambardar.
  This was known as begar. If grains are to be carried from Rohru to Rampur
  the zaildar would depute half a dozen halbandis from each village."
- **1961, Kanum**: revenue "including 'Athada Begar' or other services to the
  Primary Owner" was fixed in Sambat 1933 (about 1876 to 1877 CE).

By 1961 begar had ended. Chergaon reports "Forced labour-begar ... no longer
exists," and Shakrori reports "The bethu system has been abolished." Land did
not move in the same way in every village:

- **Chergaon**: "Out of the total 14 families of Kolis, only 5 families own
  land and the others are working as tenants to rich Thakur families."
- **Chaupal**: Kolis' "ancestors first got the land on lease ... and now
  gradually have become the owners."
- **Shakrori**: Harijans "have been allotted nautor lands on payment of a
  nominal price" but "their holdings are very small and also of an inferior
  quality." *Nautor* is waste land newly granted for cultivation.
- **Kanum**: Scheduled Castes "have to wait their turn at some distance if
  someone from the higher caste is taking water from the kuhl."
- **Chaunri**: after 1948 "all the State courts and offices located there had
  to be closed," and "the ruling family also shifted to Simla."

### Research question

> **Did the end of princely rule in 1948 change who worked for whom in five
> villages of the former Shimla Hill States? What happened to the Koli and
> other Scheduled Caste households who had carried the states' begar: did
> they gain land and leave unpaid service, or did the same division of labour
> continue as tenancy and wage work?**

Sub-questions:

1. **Before 1948.** Who owed begar, who organised it, who was exempt, and
   where did the labour go: the raja, the village deity, colonial Shimla or
   the roads?
2. **In 1961.** In each village, how do land, tenancy, occupation and access
   to water differ between Scheduled Castes and the landholding castes?
3. **Change or continuity.** Where Scheduled Caste households gained land or
   left service, what caused it: the merger, the 1953 land reform act, nautor
   grants, or road and forest wage work? Did distance from Shimla or the
   Sutlej road make a difference?

### Why the question fits both theories

The two theories predict different answers from the same evidence, so the
essay tests them against each other.

| | Annales reading | Marxist reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What explains the pattern | Physical structure. Steep terrain, small terraced plots, scarce kuhl water and isolation fix how much land exists and who can hold it. | Relations of production. Begar is surplus labour taken from a class without land, through the chain from raja to lambardar to halbandi. |
| Time scales | Long term: land, water, terrain. Medium term: Shimla's seasonal economy (1819 to 1947) and the roads. Event: the 1948 merger. | A change in the mode of extraction, from begar and service to rent and wages. |
| What 1948 should change | Little. The event sits on a structure that is still visible in 1961. | The legal form of extraction. The test is whether land moved too. |
| Supporting evidence | The same caste division of labour described in similar words in 1881, 1910, 1931 and 1961. Kuhl queues and separate water sources persisting. | Kolis moving from begar to road wage work while landholders keep the land (Chergaon), or becoming owners (Chaupal). |

### Computational method

Each method has a Programming Historian lesson, as the brief suggests.

| Method | What it measures | Lesson on programminghistorian.org | Output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Keyword-in-context concordance | How the vocabulary of labour (begar, halbandi, coolie, tenant, wage, Koli) is used in 1881, 1910, 1931 and in the five 1961 surveys | *Keywords in Context (Using n-grams) with Python*, *Counting Word Frequencies with Python* | CSV of passages, each coded as unpaid service, tenancy or wage work |
| 2. Village-by-caste dataset | Households, land owned, tenant or owner, landless, occupation, road or Simla work, kuhl access, per caste group per village | *Cleaning Data with OpenRefine*, *Creating Interactive Visualizations with Plotly* | CSV and 2 to 3 charts |
| 3. Map | Position of the five villages against Shimla, the former state boundaries and the Sutlej | *Georeferencing in QGIS 2.0* | One map, using the 1961 Punjab Census Atlas as a base |
| 4. Network of obligations (optional) | Who owed what to whom before 1948 and in 1961 | *From Hermeneutics to Data to Networks* | Two small network diagrams |
| 5. Text-reuse check (optional) | Which 1961 passages copy colonial sources such as the 1923 Bhajji settlement report | *Detecting Text Reuse with Passim* | A list of copied passages |

Method 2 carries the argument and is the slowest to build, so it starts first.

A first frequency count shows the concordance works but also shows its limit.
Rates per 10,000 words:

| Source | Words | begar/bethu | tenant | wage/labour | Koli/Dagi |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1881 | 797,251 | 0.05 | 0.60 | 0.85 | 1.83 |
| 1910 Rose | 1,131,574 | 0.08 | 0.43 | 0.53 | 1.55 |
| 1931 | 456,620 | 0.00 | 0.59 | 2.37 | 1.16 |

The layers are different kinds of document: province-wide reports against
village studies. The rates therefore cannot be read as a trend. The coded

## Stage 3. Historical interpretation (plan)

The essay argues toward one of three findings:

- **Continuity.** Begar ended in law, but land, water and tenancy still divide
  the same castes in 1961. This supports the Annales reading.
- **Transformation.** Scheduled Caste households moved from unpaid service to
  wage work and tenancy, and in some villages to ownership. This supports the
  Marxist reading of a change in the mode of extraction.
- **Divergence.** The outcome differs by village, and the dataset shows why.
  Chergaon and Chaupal already point in opposite directions.

## Section 5. Cautions

- The Barnes passage on begar describes Kangra. Ibbetson quotes it for the
  hills in general.
- No survey names the 1953 Himachal Pradesh Abolition of Big Landed Estates
  and Land Reforms Act. Pangna, outside the sample, cites it with the year
  "1963."
- The Shimla-job evidence comes from Chaunri and Shakrori. Neither survey
  gives the castes of the job holders.
- Chaunri's survey never mentions begar. Its evidence is about the merger and
  Shimla.
- The 1881 per-state tables are unreadable in the OCR.
- The 1931 census says nothing on begar or on the Praja Mandal movements.
