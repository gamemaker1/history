5 Conclusion
In the fore-going chapters an attempt has been
made to study the village life in its various aspects, social, cultural and economic. It would
appear that Nalag is a common type of hill
village with nothing to boast about. The people
are going about leisurely and although some of
them have, been working in various towns and
cities as domestic servants and rickshaw pullers
they have not been able to raise the soclal life
of the village to any appreciable extent. The
reason appears to be that they lack wherewithals
to change their made of life. They go out simply
to augment the family earnings. The village being
situated away from any motorable road has remained 'Static.' Whatever surplus food grains or
other agricultural products they have to market
thev do so at Dehar at a distance of 7 miles from
the' villag-e. This does not bring them enough
return. The result is that agricultural practices
are the same and there is no impetus to grow
any cash crop. Progress in a hill village is not
possible unless the village is connected by an
approach road or is on the main road. This
being th~ case the way of life of people of N alag
is not different from other villages similarly
situated. It is not that they are not aware of the
changes going around them but they appear to
be helpless as without economic resources they
cannot put into practice new ideas or change
their old pattern of life. It appears that unless
the approach road from Dehar is completed we
cannot expect any appreciable change in life of
the villagers.
